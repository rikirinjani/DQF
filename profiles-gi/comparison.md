# GI Drug Class Comparison — PPIs vs H2RAs vs Antacids

> A cross-class comparison of 10 GI acid-suppressive/reflux drugs across the 4-level DQF framework.

---

## Drug Set

| Sub-Class | Drugs | Mechanism |
|-----------|-------|-----------|
| **PPIs (5)** | Omeprazole, Esomeprazole, Pantoprazole, Lansoprazole, Rabeprazole | H+/K+ ATPase irreversible inhibition |
| **H2RAs (3)** | Ranitidine, Cimetidine, Famotidine | H2 receptor competitive antagonism |
| **Antacids (2)** | Al/Mg hydroxide, Ca carbonate | Direct acid neutralization |
| **Alginate (1)** | Sodium alginate | Physical raft barrier |

---

## L1 — Mechanism Comparison

| Drug | Target | Mechanism | Onset | Duration | Key L1 Feature |
|------|--------|-----------|-------|----------|----------------|
| **Omeprazole** | H+/K+ ATPase | Irreversible covalent | 3–5 d (steady state) | 24 h | CYP2C19: strongest inhibitor |
| **Esomeprazole** | H+/K+ ATPase | Irreversible covalent | 3–5 d | 24 h | Most potent acid suppression |
| **Pantoprazole** | H+/K+ ATPase | Irreversible covalent (Cys822) | 3–5 d | 24 h+ | No CYP2C19 inhibition |
| **Lansoprazole** | H+/K+ ATPase | Irreversible covalent | 3–5 d | 24 h | Highest bioavailability |
| **Rabeprazole** | H+/K+ ATPase | Irreversible covalent | 3–5 d | 24 h | CYP2C19-independent metabolism |
| **Cimetidine** | H2 receptor | Competitive | 30–60 min | 6–8 h | Broad CYP inhibition; antiandrogenic |
| **Ranitidine** | H2 receptor | Competitive | 30–60 min | 8–12 h | NDMA contamination (withdrawn) |
| **Famotidine** | H2 receptor | Competitive | 30–60 min | 10–12 h | No DDI; safest H2RA |
| **Al/Mg hydroxide** | None (chemical) | Direct neutralization | 2–10 min | 30–90 min | Chelation-based drug interactions |
| **Ca carbonate** | None (chemical) | Direct neutralization | 2–5 min | 45–90 min | Acid rebound; milk-alkali risk |
| **Na alginate** | None (physical) | Physical raft barrier | 3–10 min | 3–4 h | Non-systemic; sodium load |

### Key Insight: Onset vs Duration Trade-off

```
Onset:    Antacid < Alginate < H2RA << PPI
Duration: PPI >> H2RA > Alginate > Antacid
Healing:  PPI >> H2RA >> Alginate = Antacid
```

The fundamental trade-off in GI pharmacology: **fast onset** (antacids: 2–5 min) comes with **short duration** (30–90 min) and **no healing effect**. **Slow onset** (PPIs: 3–5 days) comes with **long duration** (24 h) and **healing of mucosal damage**.

### Key Insight: PK-PD Disconnect

PPIs show **extreme PK-PD disconnect** — plasma t½ ~1 h but 24 h acid suppression (due to covalent binding and pump recovery kinetics). H2RAs show **PK-PD concordance** — acid suppression duration roughly matches t½ and dosing interval. Antacids/alginates have **no meaningful PK** — local action only.

### Key Insight: Tolerance

**H2RAs develop tolerance** — acid suppression drops by ~20% absolute within 14 days of continuous use. This is a class-wide phenomenon unique to H2RAs (mechanism unclear: H2 receptor upregulation? histamine supersensitivity?). PPIs, antacids, and alginates do NOT develop tolerance. This is the single most important pharmacodynamic limitation of H2RAs.

| Drug | 8-week Healing Rate |
|------|-------------------|
| **Esomeprazole** | **90%** |
| **Pantoprazole** | 89% |
| **Lansoprazole** | 86% |
| **Omeprazole** | 85% |
| **Rabeprazole** | ~84% |
| **Ranitidine** | ~52% |
| **Famotidine** | ~50% |
| **Cimetidine** | ~50% |
| **Antacids** | Not indicated |
| **Alginate** | Not indicated |

**Key Insight:** PPIs achieve 84–90% healing at 8 weeks vs ~50% for H2RAs — a ~30–40% absolute difference. Antacids and alginates have no role in healing erosive disease. This is the most important clinical differentiation in the class.

### Duodenal Ulcer Healing at 4 Weeks

| Drug | 4-week Healing |
|------|---------------|
| **PPIs (all)** | ~80–85% |
| **H2RAs (all)** | ~75–85% |

**Key Insight:** For duodenal ulcer healing, PPIs and H2RAs are **comparable** — both achieve ~80% healing at 4 weeks. This is the one clinical endpoint where H2RAs match PPI efficacy.

## Framework Takeaways

1. **Three fundamentally different mechanisms** — PPIs (irreversible enzyme inhibition), H2RAs (reversible receptor antagonism), Antacids (chemical neutralization), Alginates (physical barrier). Each occupies a distinct niche that the others cannot fill.
2. **Onset vs healing trade-off is the central axis:** Fast onset = no healing (antacids, alginates). Slow onset = best healing (PPIs). H2RAs sit in the middle — moderate onset, moderate healing (ulcers only, not esophagitis).
3. **L1 structure predicts clinical profile:** PPI structures determine CYP2C19 metabolism/inhibition (pantoprazole/rabeprazole safest). Cimetidine's imidazole ring predicts CYP inhibition and antiandrogenic effects. Alginate's polymer structure predicts physical raft behavior.
4. **Tolerance is the H2RA Achilles heel:** ~20% loss of effect by day 14 makes H2RAs unsuitable for chronic therapy despite acceptable initial efficacy.
5. **Long-term PPI safety concerns are real but likely overestimated:** Observational data drives most signals; absolute excess risk <1% per patient-year for most outcomes. The NNH profile favors PPIs when the NNT for healing esophagitis is considered.
6. **Famotidine is the H2RA survivor:** After ranitidine withdrawal, famotidine is the H2RA of choice — no DDI, no antiandrogenicity, longest t½, lowest CNS risk.
