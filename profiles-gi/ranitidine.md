# Ranitidine — 4-Level Quantitative Profile

> **Role in PoC:** Most potent H2RA. Historically the most prescribed H2 antagonist. Withdrawn from global markets (2020) due to NDMA contamination — a distinct safety inflection point.

---

## L1 — Molecular Binding

### Primary Target: H2 Receptor

| Target | Ki (nM) | Functional Effect |
|--------|---------|-------------------|
| **H2 receptor** | ~140–190 nM | Competitive antagonism of histamine at gastric parietal cell |
| **H1/H3/H4 receptors** | Negligible affinity | Highly H2-selective |

Ranitidine is an **aminomethylfuran (furan) derivative** — structurally distinct from cimetidine (imidazole). It is 4–10× more potent than cimetidine at the H2 receptor and has negligible CYP inhibition, a major advantage over cimetidine.

### Off-Target Pharmacology (L3-relevant)

| Target | Potency | Relevance |
|--------|---------|-----------|
| **CYP1A2** | Negligible | No significant DDI |
| **CYP2D6** | Negligible | No significant DDI |
| **CYP3A4** | Negligible | No significant DDI |
| **CYP2C19** | Negligible | No significant DDI |
| **NDMA formation** | Process impurity (not pharmacological) | Contamination from synthesis → withdrawal |

**Key finding:** Ranitidine does NOT inhibit CYPs at clinically relevant concentrations (unlike cimetidine). Its NDMA contamination was a manufacturing impurity (not a pharmacological property), but led to global withdrawal by FDA/EMA in 2020.

### Active Metabolites

- N-oxide ranitidine (minor, via FMO)
- N-desmethyl ranitidine (minor)
- S-oxide ranitidine (minor)
- No active metabolites of clinical significance

## L2 — Pharmacokinetics

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Bioavailability** | 50–60% |
| **Dose (standard)** | 150 mg twice daily (300 mg at bedtime) |
| **Tmax** | 1–3 h |
| **Volume of distribution** | 1.2–1.9 L/kg (widely distributed) |
| **Protein binding** | 15% (low) |
| **Half-life (plasma)** | 2–3 h (extends to 4–9 h in renal impairment) |
| **Clearance** | 500–800 mL/min |
| **Metabolism** | FMO oxidation (minor); hepatic (minor: N-dealkylation, S-oxidation) |
| **Excretion** | 70–80% renal (unchanged) — major route |
| **CSF penetration** | 0.06–0.19 CSF:plasma ratio — highest among H2RAs |
| **Food effect** | Minimal effect |

**PK Signature:** Wide distribution (Vd 1.2–1.9 L/kg), low protein binding (15%), and predominantly renal elimination. The 70–80% renal excretion means dose adjustment is critical in renal impairment. CSF penetration is highest among H2RAs — explaining the higher incidence of CNS effects (confusion, delirium) in elderly patients.

---

## L3 — Systems Response

### Acid Suppression Dynamics

| Measure | Value |
|---------|-------|
| **Intragastric pH >4 holding time (day 1)** | ~38% of 24 h |
| **Onset of action** | 30–60 min (faster than PPIs) |
| **Duration (therapeutic)** | 8–12 h |
| **Tolerance development** | **Significant** — pH >4 drops by ~20% absolute by day 14 |
| **Rebound hypersecretion after withdrawal** | Yes (gastrin-mediated — less than PPIs) |

**Key finding:** H2RAs develop **tolerance** within 2–14 days — a class-wide phenomenon where the acid-suppressive effect decreases by ~20% absolute. This is the single most important clinical limitation compared to PPIs and is not seen with PPI therapy. The mechanism may involve H2 receptor upregulation or histamine supersensitivity. Tolerance is prevented by H. pylori infection.

### Tissue Penetration

- **CNS:** CSF:plasma ratio 0.06–0.19 — significant for elderly/renal patients
- **Gastric mucosa:** Good penetration at therapeutic doses
- **Note:** No parietal cell concentration mechanism (unlike PPIs which use pH trapping)

## L4 — Clinical Outcomes

### GERD and Ulcer Healing

| Endpoint | Effect |
|----------|--------|
| **GERD symptom relief vs placebo** | RR 0.77 |
| **GERD symptom relief vs PPI** | **PPIs superior** — RR 0.55 vs H2RA |
| **Erosive esophagitis healing at 8 wk** | ~52% (vs ~84% for PPIs) |
| **Duodenal ulcer healing at 4 wk** | 75–85% (comparable to PPIs) |
| **Duodenal ulcer healing at 8 wk** | 85–95% (comparable to PPIs) |
| **Maintenance of ulcer healing** | Inferior to PPIs |

### Safety / NNH

| Adverse Event | Rate | Notes |
|---------------|------|-------|
| **NDMA contamination** | N/A | Process impurity — product withdrawn 2020 |
| **CNS effects (elderly)** | ~1–5% | Confusion, delirium, hallucinations — highest among H2RAs |
| **Headache** | ~3% | Most common adverse effect |
| **GI effects** | ~2% | Diarrhea, constipation |
| **Hepatotoxicity** | Very rare | Idiosyncratic |
| **Antiplatelet effect** | No | No CYP2C19 interaction (unlike cimetidine) |

### Key Differentiator

**Most potent H2RA** — but its clinical relevance is now primarily historical due to the NDMA withdrawal. Ranitidine was the most prescribed H2RA because of its potency, lack of CYP inhibition, and twice-daily dosing. Its removal from the market in 2020 created a gap filled by famotidine (the remaining H2RA with wide availability) and PPIs.

## Framework Takeaways for Ranitidine

1. **Historical dominant H2RA, now withdrawn.** The NDMA contamination was not a pharmacological weakness but a manufacturing contamination issue. Functionally, it was the best-in-class H2RA.
2. **Tolerance is the class Achilles heel.** The ~20% drop in acid suppression over 14 days means H2RAs lose their advantage precisely when continued therapy is most needed.
3. **PPIs are superior for erosive disease.** The ~52% vs ~84% EE healing rate at 8 weeks makes the superiority of PPIs unequivocal for moderate-severe GERD.
4. **Duodenal ulcer healing is the one area where H2RAs match PPIs** — both achieve ~80% healing at 4 weeks.
