# Lansoprazole — 4-Level Quantitative Profile

> **Role in PoC:** Highest bioavailability (80–85%). Unique biliary excretion. Most potent CYP2C19 inhibitor in vitro — distinct DDI concern.

---

## L1 — Molecular Binding

### Primary Target: H+/K+ ATPase (proton pump)

| Target | Ki (nM) | Functional Effect |
|--------|---------|-------------------|
| **H+/K+ ATPase — Cys813** | Covalent (irreversible) | Blocks gastric acid secretion |
| **H+/K+ ATPase — Cys321** | Covalent (luminal vestibule) | Glutathione-reversible binding |

Lansoprazole binds Cys321 in the luminal vestibule (unique among PPIs), which is accessible to glutathione reduction — giving it the shortest pump recovery half-life (~13 h, vs ~46 h pantoprazole, ~27 h omeprazole).

### Off-Target Pharmacology (L3-relevant)

| Target | Potency | Relevance |
|--------|---------|-----------|
| **CYP2C19** | Ki **0.4–1.5 µM** (most potent inhibitor in vitro) | Strongest DDI potential — clopidogrel, diazepam, phenytoin |
| **Anti-inflammatory** | ↓ IL-8, ↓ CINC-1 in animal models | Most studied for pleiotropic anti-inflammatory effects |

**Key finding:** Lansoprazole has the **most potent in vitro CYP2C19 inhibition** among all PPIs (Ki 0.4–1.5 µM), even stronger than omeprazole (Ki 2–6 µM). Despite this, clinical DDI studies show the interaction is similar to omeprazole in magnitude — likely due to lansoprazole's higher bioavailability partially compensating for the lower required dose.

### Active Metabolites
- 5-Hydroxylansoprazole (active via CYP2C19)
- Lansoprazole sulfone (inactive via CYP3A4)

## L2 — Pharmacokinetics

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Bioavailability** | **80–85%** (highest among all PPIs) |
| **Dose (standard)** | 30 mg once daily (15 mg maintenance) |
| **Tmax** | 1.7 h |
| **Volume of distribution** | 0.4 L/kg |
| **Protein binding** | 97% |
| **Half-life (plasma)** | 1.6 h |
| **Clearance** | 400–650 mL/min |
| **Metabolism** | CYP2C19 (>80%) + CYP3A4 |
| **PK pattern** | Linear |
| **Excretion** | 14–23% urinary (biliary excretion is unique — significant) |
| **CYP2C19 genotype effect** | Major (>80% CYP2C19-dependent) |

**PK Signature:** Highest bioavailability (80–85%) means the most consistent oral absorption — less affected by fed/fasted state variability. Unique biliary excretion contributes to enterohepatic recirculation. The 30 mg dose is bioequivalent to omeprazole 20 mg in standard pharmacodynamic endpoints.

*Sources: El Rouby 2018 (PMID:29620484); Li 2004 (PMID:15258107).*

## L3 — Systems Response

### Acid Suppression Dynamics

| Measure | Value |
|---------|-------|
| **pH >4 holding time (day 5, 30 mg)** | 11.5 h (47.9% of 24 h) |
| **Relative potency (vs omeprazole)** | 0.90× |
| **Onset of action** | Moderate |
| **Pump recovery half-life** | **~13 h** (shortest — glutathione-reversible Cys321 binding) |

Lansoprazole provides acid suppression comparable to omeprazole (11.5 h vs 11.8 h pH >4). The short recovery half-life means more rapid return of acid secretion after discontinuation — but also less risk of profound sustained hypochlorhydria.

## L4 — Clinical Outcomes

### Erosive Esophagitis Healing

| Timepoint | Healing Rate (95% CI) |
|-----------|----------------------|
| **4 weeks** | 73% (67–79%) |
| **8 weeks** | 86% (83–90%) |
| **vs omeprazole 20 mg at 4 wk** | OR 1.21 (0.96–1.51) — not significant |

### Maintenance

| Endpoint | Value |
|----------|-------|
| **Maintenance at 12 months** | 55–90% (wide range across studies) |
| **Long-term (6 year) maintenance** | 75% at 72 months (Kovacs 2008) |

### Key Differentiators

1. **Highest bioavailability** — most consistent absorption regardless of food timing
2. **Biliary excretion** — unique elimination pathway
3. **Most potent CYP2C19 inhibitor in vitro** — theoretically highest DDI potential, though clinical effect similar to omeprazole
4. **Shortest pump recovery** (~13 h) — fastest return to baseline acid secretion
5. **Available as dexlansoprazole** — R-enantiomer with dual delayed-release (DDR) formulation for extended duration

## Framework Takeaways for Lansoprazole

1. **Highest bioavailability is its best practical feature:** 80–85% oral absorption means less variability and more predictable effect than omeprazole (30–40%).
2. **CYP2C19 inhibition is potent on paper but similar clinically:** Despite Ki 0.4 µM in vitro, the clinical DDI magnitude is similar to omeprazole — likely because the standard dose (30 mg) is lower on a mg basis.
3. **Short pump recovery is a dual-edged feature:** Faster return to baseline is good for tolerability but means less forgiveness for missed doses.
