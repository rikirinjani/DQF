# Cimetidine — 4-Level Quantitative Profile

> **Role in PoC:** First-in-class H2RA. Highest DDI burden (CYP inhibition) and unique antiandrogenic effects — the safety and DDI cautionary tale among GI drugs.

---

## L1 — Molecular Binding

### Primary Target: H2 Receptor

| Target | Ki (nM) | Functional Effect |
|--------|---------|-------------------|
| **H2 receptor** | ~590 nM | Competitive antagonism |
| **H1/H3/H4 receptors** | Negligible | Highly H2-selective |

Cimetidine is an **imidazole derivative** — the structural class that also gives it CYP-chelating properties. It is the least potent H2RA (1× reference), 4–10× less potent than ranitidine and 20–50× less potent than famotidine.

### Off-Target Pharmacology (L3-relevant) — **Major Differentiator**

| Target | Potency | Relevance |
|--------|---------|-----------|
| **Androgen receptor** | Competitive DHT displacement | Gynecomastia, impotence, reduced libido — **unique among H2RAs** |
| **CYP1A2** | Ki 4.58 µM | Theophylline, caffeine, olanzapine interaction |
| **CYP2C9** | Weak competitive | Warfarin interaction (minor) |
| **CYP2C19** | Ki 8.22 µM | Diazepam, phenytoin, clopidogrel interaction |
| **CYP2D6** | Ki 3.5 µM | Metoprolol, desipramine, antidepressants interaction |
| **CYP3A4** | Ki 11 µM | Midazolam, triazolam, nifedipine interaction |

**Key finding:** Cimetidine inhibits **5 CYP isozymes** at clinically relevant concentrations — unique among H2RAs (ranitidine and famotidine have negligible CYP inhibition). The imidazole ring chelates the heme iron of CYP enzymes. This creates a broad DDI profile that makes cimetidine impractical in polypharmacy patients.

### Active Metabolites
- Cimetidine sulfoxide (minor)
- Hydroxycimetidine (minor)
- No active metabolites of clinical significance

## L2 — Pharmacokinetics

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Bioavailability** | ~60% |
| **Dose (standard)** | 400 mg twice daily (800 mg at bedtime) |
| **Tmax** | 1–2 h |
| **Volume of distribution** | 0.8–1.2 L/kg |
| **Protein binding** | 15–20% (low) |
| **Half-life (plasma)** | ~2 h (extends to 4–6 h in elderly, ~8 h in renal impairment) |
| **Clearance** | 400–650 mL/min |
| **Metabolism** | Hepatic (minor: sulfoxidation, hydroxylation) — also inhibits own metabolism |
| **Excretion** | 50–70% renal (unchanged) |
| **CSF penetration** | 0.1–0.24 CSF:plasma ratio |
| **Auto-inhibition** | Yes — cimetidine inhibits its own CYP metabolism (non-linear PK) |

**PK Signature:** Moderate bioavailability, extensive renal elimination, and auto-inhibition (cimetidine slows its own clearance via CYP3A4 inhibition). The short t½ (~2 h) necessitates more frequent dosing (typically BID-QID) compared to newer H2RAs.

## L3 — Systems Response

### Acid Suppression Dynamics

| Measure | Value |
|---------|-------|
| **Intragastric pH >4 holding time (day 1)** | ~35–40% of 24 h at standard dose |
| **Onset of action** | 30–60 min |
| **Duration (therapeutic)** | 6–8 h |
| **Tolerance development** | Yes — class effect (~20% drop by day 14) |

### Downstream Effects (Unique to Cimetidine)

| Effect | Mechanism | Clinical Impact |
|--------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Gynecomastia** | Androgen receptor blockade | 0.2–2% incidence (dose-dependent); reversible on discontinuation |
| **Impotence / reduced libido** | Androgen receptor blockade | Rare at standard doses; more common at high doses (e.g., Zollinger-Ellison) |
| **Prolactin elevation** | Possible H2-mediated | Mild |
| **CYP inhibition cascade** | Multiple CYP isozymes | Affects clearance of 30+ drugs |

## L4 — Clinical Outcomes

### Peptic Ulcer Healing

| Endpoint | Effect |
|----------|--------|
| **Duodenal ulcer healing at 4 wk** | 70–80% (800 mg/day) |
| **Duodenal ulcer healing at 8 wk** | 85–95% |
| **Gastric ulcer healing at 8 wk** | 60–70% |
| **Maintenance therapy** | 400 mg at bedtime — reduces relapse rate |

### GERD

| Endpoint | Effect |
|----------|--------|
| **GERD symptom relief vs placebo** | RR ~0.75 |
| **Erosive esophagitis healing** | Poor — ~50% at 8 wk (PPIs ~84%) |
| **Nocturnal heartburn relief** | Effective (bedtime dose targets nocturnal acid breakthrough) |

### Safety / NNH

| Adverse Event | Incidence | Notes |
|---------------|-----------|-------|
| **DDI (broad CYP inhibition)** | Clinically significant in polypharmacy | Theophylline, warfarin, phenytoin, many others — dose adjustment needed |
| **Gynecomastia** | 0.2–2% | Dose-dependent, reversible |
| **CNS effects (elderly)** | ~1–3% | Confusion, delirium — less than ranitidine |
| **Diarrhea** | ~1% | Most common GI effect |
| **Headache** | ~1% | |
| **Neutropenia** | Very rare | Reversible |

### Key Differentiator

**First-in-class, highest DDI burden, unique antiandrogenic effects.** Cimetidine is now rarely used in clinical practice because:
1. **Broad CYP inhibition** — affected 30+ drugs (warfarin, theophylline, phenytoin, clopidogrel, many others)
2. **Antiandrogenic effects** — gynecomastia, impotence (unique among H2RAs)
3. **Famotidine is superior** — more potent, safer, longer t½, no DDI, no antiandrogenic effects

Its historical importance is as the first H2RA (Nobel Prize 1988 to Black, who developed the H2 receptor concept) and as the class prototype.

## Framework Takeaways for Cimetidine

1. **The DDI cautionary tale:** Cimetidine's imidazole structure was the price of being first-in-class — the CYP-chelating property created a DDI burden that made it clinically impractical once alternatives existed. This is a textbook example of L1 structure predicting L4 clinical limitations.
2. **Antiandrogenic effects are invisible without L1 profiling:** A single-score ranking would never surface that cimetidine uniquely affects androgen signaling. Only L1 off-target profiling captures this.
3. **Famotidine is superior in every dimension except historical importance** — more potent, no DDI, no antiandrogenic, longer t½, better safety.
