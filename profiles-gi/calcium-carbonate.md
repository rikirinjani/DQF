# Calcium Carbonate — 4-Level Quantitative Profile

> **Role in PoC:** Fastest chemical neutralization among antacids. Dual role as calcium supplement creates unique risks (milk-alkali syndrome). Unique acid rebound phenomenon — not seen with other antacids.

---

## L1 — Mechanism (Direct Chemical Neutralization)

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| **Reaction** | CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂ |
| **Neutralization speed** | Fastest — ~40 sec to reach pH 3 |
| **Neutralizing capacity** | 10–20 mEq per gram |
| **CO₂ production** | Yes — causes belching, bloating |
| **Onset** | 2–5 min |
| **Duration** | 30–60 min (fasted) to 2 h (fed) |

Calcium carbonate is the most potent and fastest neutralizing antacid on a per-gram basis. The CO₂ byproduct produces a distinctive belching sensation that some patients find reassuring ("I can feel it working") and others find bothersome.

### Key Chemistry

- Fine particle size → rapid dissolution in gastric acid
- Solubility decreases as pH rises (self-limiting neutralization)
- Reacts with dietary phosphate → used as phosphate binder in CKD (but concern for Ca overload)

---

## L2 — Pharmacokinetics

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Calcium absorption** | ~10–20% (pH-dependent; increases with achlorhydria) |
| **Onset of neutralization** | 2–5 min |
| **Duration of action** | 30 min (fasted) to 2 h (fed) |
| **Peak serum Ca²⁺** | 2–4 h after oral dose |
| **Renal calcium excretion** | Increases with dose |
| **Vitamin D interaction** | Ca absorption increased by Vit D |

**PK Signature:** Unlike Al(OH)₃/Mg(OH)₂, calcium carbonate has **significant systemic absorption** (~10–20%). In patients with CKD or high intake, this can produce hypercalcemia, hypercalciuria, and nephrocalcinosis. The absorbed calcium fraction also suppresses PTH and increases renal calcium load.

### Drug Interactions

| Interaction | Mechanism | Severity |
|-------------|-----------|----------|
| **Tetracyclines** | Chelation | Major — separate by 2 h |
| **Fluoroquinolones** | Chelation | Major — separate by 2 h |
| **Iron preparations** | Chelation | Major — separate by 2 h |
| **Thyroid hormone** | Reduced absorption | Moderate — separate by 4 h |
| **Thiazide diuretics** | Synergistic hypercalcemia | Major — avoid combination |
| **Digoxin** | Toxicity risk (Ca + digoxin) | Moderate — monitor in hypercalcemia |

---

## L3 — Systems Response

### Gastric pH Effect

| Measure | Value |
|---------|-------|
| **Intragastric pH elevation** | pH 2→5 within 5 min |
| **Duration pH >3** | 45–90 min |
| **Acid rebound** | **Yes — unique among antacids** |
| **Peak rebound** | 1–2 h after neutralization ceases |
| **Mechanism of rebound** | Gastrin-mediated (Ca²⁺ stimulates gastrin release from G-cells) |

**Key finding:** Calcium carbonate is the **only antacid that produces clinically significant acid rebound** — the rise in intragastric pH releases somatostatin inhibition on G-cells, and Ca²⁺ directly stimulates gastrin secretion. The resulting gastrin surge produces rebound acid secretion that can exceed baseline levels, creating a cycle of use → relief → rebound → more use.

### Downstream Effects

| Effect | Mechanism | Clinical |
|--------|-----------|----------|
| **Milk-alkali syndrome** | Ca²⁺ absorption + metabolic alkalosis | Hypercalcemia, nephrocalcinosis, renal failure — classic triad |
| **Nephrolithiasis** | Increased urinary Ca²⁺ excretion | Calcium oxalate stones |
| **Calcium overload (CKD)** | Reduced renal Ca²⁺ clearance | Soft tissue calcification, cardiovascular calcification |
| **Constipation** | Calcium soaps in colon | More constipating than Al(OH)₃ |

## L4 — Clinical Outcomes

### Indications

| Indication | Effect |
|------------|--------|
| **Heartburn (symptom relief)** | Excellent — fastest onset (2–5 min) |
| **GERD symptom relief** | Effective for mild/intermittent |
| **Calcium supplementation** | Effective — 500–1000 mg elemental Ca per day |
| **Erosive esophagitis** | Not indicated — no healing effect |
| **Phosphate binder (CKD)** | Alternative to sevelamer — Ca overload risk |

### Safety / NNH

| Adverse Event | Rate | Notes |
|---------------|------|-------|
| **Acid rebound** | Common with regular use | Unique to CaCO₃ among antacids |
| **Milk-alkali syndrome** | Chronic high-dose use | 1.5–3 g/day threshold; classic triad |
| **Constipation** | Common | Worse than Al(OH)₃ |
| **Kidney stones** | Increased long-term risk | ~1–1.5× increased risk vs no supplement |
| **Belching/bloating** | ~5–10% | CO₂ byproduct |
| **Hypercalcemia (CKD)** | Significant in stage 4–5 | May accelerate vascular calcification |

### Key Differentiator

**Fastest chemical neutralization, but most complex safety profile among antacids.** The dual role as both antacid and calcium supplement creates unique risks:
1. **Acid rebound** — unique among antacids; drives dependency
2. **Milk-alkali syndrome** — classic triad of hypercalcemia, alkalosis, renal impairment
3. **Kidney stones** — increased risk with long-term use
4. **CV calcification risk** — in CKD patients

These risks make calcium carbonate best suited for **occasional, symptomatic use** rather than chronic daily therapy.

## Framework Takeaways for Calcium Carbonate

1. **Fastest neutralization, most complex rebound:** The unique acid rebound phenomenon (Ca²⁺-mediated gastrin release) makes CaCO₃ more likely to cause a dependency cycle than other antacids.
2. **Dual role as calcium supplement is both asset and liability:** Useful for meeting calcium needs but creates risks (milk-alkali syndrome, nephrolithiasis, CV calcification in CKD) that other antacids don't have.
3. **Best for occasional/PRN use:** For patients who take antacids once a month for meal-related heartburn, CaCO₃ is fine. For daily users, Al(OH)₃/Mg(OH)₂ or a PPI is safer.
4. **Contraindicated in CKD:** The calcium and alkaloid loads are unsafe in patients with reduced renal function.
