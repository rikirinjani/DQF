# Aluminum/Magnesium Hydroxide — 4-Level Quantitative Profile

> **Role in PoC:** Classic combination antacid with balanced bowel effects. Highest neutralizing capacity. Prototype for direct acid neutralization — no receptor pharmacology, no systemic absorption concern in normal renal function.

---

## L1 — Mechanism (Direct Chemical Neutralization)

Unlike PPIs and H2RAs, antacids have **no receptor target**. Their mechanism is direct chemical neutralization of gastric acid.

| Component | Reaction | Effect |
|-----------|----------|--------|
| **Al(OH)₃** | Al(OH)₃ + 3HCl → AlCl₃ + 3H₂O | Slow, sustained neutralization |
| **Mg(OH)₂** | Mg(OH)₂ + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + 2H₂O | Rapid neutralization |
| **Combination** | Balanced formulation | Offset bowel effects |
| **Pepsin inhibition** | pH-dependent precipitation | Reduces pepsin activity at pH >4 |

**Neutralizing capacity:** 25–50 mEq per dose (varies by formulation). Onset: 2–5 min. Duration: 30–60 min (fasted) to 2–3 h (fed).

### Key Chemistry

| Property | Al(OH)₃ | Mg(OH)₂ |
|----------|---------|---------|
| **Reaction speed** | Slow | Fast |
| **Bowel effect** | Constipating | Laxative |
| **Systemic absorption** | Minimal (~0.01%) | Minimal (~5–10% Mg absorbed) |
| **Onset** | 5–10 min | 2–5 min |
| **Duration** | 60–90 min | 30–60 min |

## L2 — Pharmacokinetics

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Absorption (Al)** | <0.01% — primarily local action |
| **Absorption (Mg)** | 5–10% (renally cleared) |
| **Tmax (systemic)** | N/A (local action) |
| **Onset of neutralization** | 2–10 min |
| **Duration of action** | 30 min (fasted) to 2–3 h (fed) |
| **Aluminum clearance** | Renal (accumulates in CKD) |
| **Magnesium clearance** | Renal |
| **Safety in pregnancy** | Generally safe (Category B/C) |

**PK Signature:** Minimal systemic absorption in normal renal function. Aluminum accumulates in bone and CNS with chronic use in CKD (dialysis encephalopathy). Magnesium accumulates in CKD → hypermagnesemia (hypotension, respiratory depression). The short duration of action is the main clinical limitation — dosing is required QID or PRN.

### Drug Interactions

| Interaction | Mechanism | Severity |
|-------------|-----------|----------|
| **Tetracyclines** | Chelation | Major — separate by 2 h |
| **Fluoroquinolones** | Chelation | Major — separate by 2 h |
| **Iron preparations** | Chelation | Moderate — separate by 2 h |
| **Thyroid hormone** | Reduced absorption | Moderate — separate by 4 h |
| **Bisphosphonates** | Chelation | Major — separate by 2 h |
| **Digoxin** | Reduced absorption | Minor — monitor |

## L3 — Systems Response

### Gastric pH Effect

| Measure | Value |
|---------|-------|
| **Intragastric pH elevation** | pH 2→5 within 5 min (fed state) |
| **Duration pH >3** | 60–90 min |
| **Duration pH >4** | 30–60 min |
| **Effect on gastric emptying** | Delayed (Al) / Accelerated (Mg) |
| **Acid rebound** | No (unlike CaCO₃) |

### Downstream Effects

| Effect | Mechanism | Clinical |
|--------|-----------|----------|
| **Aluminum accumulation** | Absorbed Al³⁺ deposited in bone/brain | Osteomalacia, encephalopathy in CKD |
| **Hyperphosphatemia (Al)** | Al binds dietary phosphate | Used as phosphate binder in CKD |
| **Hypermagnesemia** | Mg²⁺ accumulation in CKD | Hypotension, respiratory depression, cardiac arrest |
| **Bowel effects** | Al slows, Mg accelerates | Balanced in combination products |

## L4 — Clinical Outcomes

### Indications

| Indication | Effect |
|------------|--------|
| **Heartburn (symptom relief)** | Effective — onset within 5 min |
| **GERD symptom relief** | Effective for mild/intermittent symptoms |
| **Dyspepsia** | Effective for meal-related symptoms |
| **Erosive esophagitis healing** | **Not indicated** — no healing effect |
| **Peptic ulcer** | Controversial — minimal evidence for healing |
| **Prevention of stress ulcers** | Not recommended (PPI preferred) |

### Safety / NNH

| Adverse Event | Rate | Notes |
|---------------|------|-------|
| **Constipation (Al alone)** | Common | Mg counterbalances in combo |
| **Diarrhea (Mg alone)** | Common | Al counterbalances in combo |
| **Aluminum osteomalacia** | Chronic use, CKD | Long-term use in renal failure |
| **Aluminum encephalopathy** | Dialysis patients | Historical — now rare with monitoring |
| **Hypermagnesemia** | CKD | Rare with normal renal function |

### Key Differentiator

**Fastest onset of all GI drugs** (2–10 min vs 30–60 min for H2RAs, 3–5 days for PPIs). The trade-off: short duration (30–60 min), no healing effect, drug interaction burden (chelation), and safety concerns in CKD.

## Framework Takeaways for Al(OH)₃/Mg(OH)₂

1. **Mechanism fundamentally different from PPIs/H2RAs:** Direct neutralization, not receptor pharmacology. The shortest onset but also the shortest duration.
2. **No healing effect:** Antacids relieve symptoms but do not heal erosive esophagitis or peptic ulcers. This is the most important clinical distinction from PPIs and H2RAs.
3. **Safety in CKD is the main concern:** Aluminum and magnesium both require renal clearance. In patients with even mild CKD, antacid accumulation is a real concern.
4. **Drug chelation is frequently overlooked:** The 2-hour separation requirement for multiple drug classes is a significant practical adherence burden.
