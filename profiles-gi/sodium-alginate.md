# Sodium Alginate — 4-Level Quantitative Profile

> **Role in PoC:** Unique non-systemic mechanism — physical raft barrier, not acid neutralization. Best for regurgitation-dominant GERD. Safest GI drug in pregnancy.

---

## L1 — Mechanism (Physical Barrier Formation)

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| **Mechanism** | Alginate + NaHCO₃ → CO₂ → raft floats on gastric contents |
| **Raft pH** | ~5–6 (near neutral) |
| **Raft longevity** | 3–4 h in stomach |
| **Raft thickness** | 1–2 cm (coverage of gastro-esophageal junction) |
| **Neutralizing capacity** | Low (limited — not the primary mechanism) |
| **Onset** | 3–5 min (raft forms in stomach) |
| **Duration** | 3–4 h (outlasts traditional antacids) |

Sodium alginate is extracted from brown seaweed (Phaeophyceae). In the presence of gastric acid and NaHCO₃, it forms a low-density alginate gel "raft" that floats on the gastric contents. The raft physically displaces the "acid pocket" (layer of undigested acid that sits above food in the proximal stomach) away from the gastro-esophageal junction.

**Key mechanistic distinction:** Unlike antacids that neutralize acid, alginate creates a **physical barrier** that prevents reflux. It is classified as an alginate-antacid combination (typically with K₂CO₃ or CaCO₃ as raft-hardening agents).

### Key Chemistry

- Alginic acid + NaHCO₃ + Ca²⁺ (optional) → cross-linked gel matrix
- Raft formation requires sufficient gastric volume (>50 mL)
- Less effective in upright position (gravity competes with raft)
- Not degraded by gastric acid or pepsin

## L2 — Pharmacokinetics

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Systemic absorption** | **Minimal** (<0.5%) — essentially non-systemic |
| **Sodium content** | **~50–60% of WHO daily limit at 4× daily dosing** |
| **Onset of action** | 3–10 min |
| **Raft duration** | 3–4 h |
| **Raft elimination** | Gastric emptying (raft empties with meal) |
| **Renal clearance of absorbed fraction** | Not clinically significant |
| **CYP metabolism** | None (non-systemic) |
| **Pregnancy safety** | **Category B — safest GI drug class in pregnancy** |

**PK Signature:** Essentially non-systemic — no absorption, no metabolism, no CYP involvement. The primary systemic concern is sodium content (~110–140 mg Na per dose; 4× daily = 440–560 mg, ~50–60% of WHO 2 g/day limit). This is clinically relevant in hypertensive or CKD patients.

## L3 — Systems Response

### Raft Dynamics

| Measure | Value |
|---------|-------|
| **Intragastric pH** | Limited effect (unlike antacids) |
| **Esophageal pH** | Raft reduces acid pocket exposure to lower esophagus |
| **Displacement of acid pocket** | Yes — physical barrier, not pH change |
| **Effect on number of reflux events** | Reduces count and volume |
| **Effect on proximal migration** | Limits height of refluxate |
| **Postprandial use** | Most effective after meals |
| **Combination effect** | Enhanced by K₂CO₃/NaHCO₃ (alginate-antacid formulations) |

### Downstream Effects

| Effect | Mechanism | Clinical |
|--------|-----------|----------|
| **Sodium load** | ~110–140 mg per dose | Relevant for hypertension, CKD, heart failure |
| **Calcium load** | Some formulations | Minimal compared to CaCO₃ |
| **GI transit** | Not affected by raft | Raft empties with gastric contents |
| **Drug absorption** | Minimal risk | Raft is physical, not chemical (unlike chelating antacids) |

## L4 — Clinical Outcomes

### GERD Symptom Relief

| Endpoint | Effect |
|----------|--------|
| **Heartburn relief vs placebo** | RR ~0.70 (moderate effect) |
| **Heartburn relief vs antacid** | Comparable or slightly better |
| **Regurgitation reduction** | **Superior to antacids** — physical barrier is unique advantage |
| **Onset of relief** | 3–10 min (slower than antacids' 2–5 min) |
| **Duration of relief** | 3–4 h (longer than antacids' 30–60 min) |
| **Erosive esophagitis healing** | **Not indicated** — no healing effect |
| **PPI add-on therapy** | Effective for breakthrough regurgitation on PPI |

### Key Differentiator

**Unique mechanism among GI drugs.** Alginate is the only GI drug that works by **physical barrier formation** rather than acid neutralization (antacids) or acid suppression (H2RAs, PPIs).

| Dimension | Alginate vs Antacid | Alginate vs PPI |
|-----------|-------------------|-----------------|
| Onset | Similar (3–10 min) | Much faster |
| Duration | **Longer** (3–4 h vs 30–60 min) | Shorter (vs 24 h) |
| Regurgitation | **Superior** | **Superior to PPI** |
| Healing | None | None |
| Safety | **No drug interactions** | Safer than long-term PPI |

## Framework Takeaways for Sodium Alginate

1. **Mechanism is unique:** The physical raft mechanism is fundamentally different from all other GI drugs. It targets the physical process of reflux (regurgitation), not the chemistry of acid.
2. **Best for regurgitation-dominant GERD:** PPIs reduce acid but don't stop reflux events. Alginates physically prevent the refluxate from reaching the esophagus. For patients whose primary symptom is regurgitation (not heartburn), alginate is superior.
3. **Safest in pregnancy:** Non-systemic mechanism means no fetal exposure. All pregnancy guideline bodies recommend alginates as first-line for GERD in pregnancy.
4. **Complementary to PPIs:** In patients who still have breakthrough symptoms on PPI therapy, adding alginate postprandially addresses the mechanical component of reflux that PPIs cannot fix.
5. **Sodium content is the main limitation:** In patients with hypertension, CKD, or heart failure, the cumulative sodium load from 4× daily dosing may be clinically significant.
